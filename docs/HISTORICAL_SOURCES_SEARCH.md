# Historical-geography source search: deliverables A–S

Generated by `scripts/historical-data/discover/report.py` from the files listed at the end. Scope: the map only. Built 2026-10-01.

**What this cycle did, in short.** Search enormous, filter rigorously, integrate selectively, validate obsessively, then search again: 1,901,758 catalogue records were harvested from 19 channels (DataCite, OpenAIRE, data.europa.eu, Zenodo, ArcGIS Hub, PANGAEA, Europeana, David Rumsey, 84 Dataverse installations, re3data, OpenGeoMetadata university catalogues, ARIADNE collections, discovery chains) with 4,991 systematic multilingual queries; duplicates merged to **379,246 unique candidates**; every candidate carries an investigation state; **6,969** went beyond the catalogue record (repository file lists, data samples, or a reviewed decision); the strongest were acquired and integrated through a reusable spec-driven loader; coverage was re-measured. Cells below 70 went from **6,019** to **5,247** of 8,730. The discovery inventory and the integrated data are separate deliverables (T below, and the map).

**Rules followed throughout.** A record is dated only by what its own source says about it: a construction window, a period its record names, the year a register or map lists it (a *snapshot* — evidence it existed then, nothing about before or after), or a first attestation (never treated as a founding). A dataset's overall period is never copied onto its records. Conflicting dates are kept side by side. Undated records stay undated (drawn only when the reader asks for undated records, never counted in coverage).

**Recommended generic mechanism.** Keep three levels: *source record* (as published, never edited), *entity* (a place through time, with an id of its own) and *map representation* (what is drawn for a given year). Records join an entity by evidence, in order: a shared external identifier (Wikidata, Pleiades, GeoNames, national register ids); a shared name form (any recorded name, normalised) within a distance that depends on the kind of place; a link stated by a source (e.g. a gazetteer that cites another). A join is a stored claim with its evidence, reversible, and never merges two records of the same dataset. Names stay attached to their records with their own dates, so the label for a year is chosen from the entity's name forms valid then — no rule like "Istanbul = Constantinople" is hard-coded; it falls out of the dated name forms.
